## Two things I need you to decide

**1. The women's risk figure contradicts itself across the site.**

The homepage hero says *1 in 3 Irish women … over 60*. The research page (Athena / Empathy Research 2023) says *1 in 2 women & 1 in 5 men over the age of 50 are at risk from osteoporosis*, sourced to getthestandalone.ie. Different denominators, different ages, same site. If we post both we look sloppy, and someone will screenshot it.

I have built the cards on the **homepage hero figure (1 in 3 women, 1 in 5 men, over 60)** because it is the site's headline claim and the more conservative of the two. Worth flagging to the client that the site itself needs reconciling.

**2. The fragility fracture definition — the one she flagged.**

Your note says the website needs updating. It has already been updated. The live homepage now reads:

> "A fragility fracture is a broken bone caused by a force that would not normally break a healthy bone — such as a minor fall, a bump, or even everyday activities."

That is better than the version in your list (it covers bumps and everyday activity, not only falls from standing height), and it matches the IOF definition. I have used the live site's wording on the card. If she wanted a *different* change to that page, I need to know what it is.

## Rewritten for accuracy

- **"Calcium is the most important mineral for bone health"** → softened to *"The skeleton stores 99% of the body's calcium"*, which is the site's own line and is a fact rather than a ranking. Calling calcium "most important" invites an argument about vitamin D, protein, and vitamin K, and it isn't a claim the site makes.
- **"Dairy products are the richest and most bioavailable sources of calcium in the Irish diet"** → reframed as *"Dairy is the richest and most readily absorbed source of calcium in the Irish diet"*, and paired in the caption with non-dairy options. As written, the original reads as dairy-industry copy on a post that also targets vegans, and it clashes with the "are you vegan?" risk card.
- **"Low calcium = low bone density"** → replaced with the site's mechanism, which is stronger and defensible: *"When you don't eat enough calcium, your body takes it from your bones."*
